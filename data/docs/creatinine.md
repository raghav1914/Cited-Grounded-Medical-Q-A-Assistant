# Creatinine test

Creatinine is a waste product made by your muscles as part of normal daily activity. Healthy kidneys filter creatinine out of the blood, and it leaves the body in urine. A creatinine blood test measures how much creatinine is in your blood and is used to check how well your kidneys are working.

## Why the test is done

The test may be ordered as part of a routine health check, or if a health professional suspects a kidney problem. Symptoms that may lead to the test include tiredness, swelling in the feet or ankles, changes in how often you urinate, and high blood pressure.

## Normal results

A normal creatinine level depends on the laboratory, your age, sex, and how much muscle you have. As a general guide, typical adult blood creatinine values are about 0.7 to 1.3 mg/dL (62 to 115 micromoles/L) for men and about 0.6 to 1.1 mg/dL (53 to 97 micromoles/L) for women. Different laboratories may use slightly different reference ranges, so results should always be compared against the range printed on your own lab report.

## What higher-than-normal results can mean

A creatinine level above the normal range can be a sign that the kidneys are not working as well as they should. Possible causes include kidney disease, reduced blood flow to the kidneys, dehydration, a blockage of the urinary tract, or certain medicines. A high result does not by itself diagnose a specific condition — it is interpreted together with other tests and your overall health by a health professional.

## What lower-than-normal results can mean

Lower-than-normal creatinine is less common and is usually not a cause for concern. It can happen with conditions that reduce muscle mass or during pregnancy.

Because reference ranges and interpretation vary, discuss your specific result with a healthcare provider rather than relying on the number alone.
