# High blood pressure (hypertension)

Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing against the walls of the arteries as the heart pumps blood. Hypertension, also called high blood pressure, is when this pressure is too high. It is a common condition and a major cause of premature death worldwide.

Blood pressure is written as two numbers. The first (systolic) number is the pressure in the arteries when the heart beats. The second (diastolic) number is the pressure when the heart rests between beats. Blood pressure is measured in millimetres of mercury (mmHg).

## When is blood pressure considered high?

Hypertension is generally diagnosed when blood pressure readings on two different days are consistently at or above 140/90 mmHg. A normal blood pressure level is around 120/80 mmHg. Readings between these values are sometimes described as elevated or as raised risk.

## Symptoms

Most people with hypertension have no symptoms at all, which is why it is sometimes called a "silent" condition. Because of this, the only reliable way to know is to have blood pressure measured. When blood pressure is very high, some people may notice headaches, chest pain, dizziness, difficulty breathing, or blurred vision.

## Risk factors

Risk factors that can be changed include unhealthy diets (especially high salt intake), physical inactivity, tobacco and alcohol use, and being overweight. Risk factors that cannot be changed include a family history of hypertension, being older than 65, and having other conditions such as diabetes or kidney disease.

## Prevention and management

Lifestyle changes can help lower blood pressure: reducing salt intake, eating more fruit and vegetables, being physically active, avoiding tobacco, and reducing alcohol. When lifestyle changes are not enough, medicines may be prescribed by a health professional. Uncontrolled high blood pressure can lead to heart attack, stroke, kidney damage, and other serious problems, so regular monitoring is important.
