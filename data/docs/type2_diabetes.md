# Diabetes

Diabetes is a chronic condition that occurs when the body cannot properly use the sugar (glucose) in the blood. This happens either because the pancreas does not produce enough insulin, or because the body cannot effectively use the insulin it produces. Insulin is a hormone that helps glucose move from the blood into the body's cells for energy. Over time, high blood glucose can damage the heart, blood vessels, eyes, kidneys, and nerves.

## Types of diabetes

Type 1 diabetes is caused by the body producing little or no insulin, and people with it need daily insulin. Type 2 diabetes, the most common form, occurs when the body becomes resistant to insulin or does not make enough of it. It is largely linked to excess body weight and physical inactivity. Gestational diabetes is high blood glucose that develops during pregnancy.

## Symptoms

Symptoms of diabetes can include feeling very thirsty, needing to urinate more often than usual, blurred vision, feeling tired, and unintentional weight loss. For type 2 diabetes these symptoms may be mild or absent for a long time, so the condition can go undiagnosed for years.

## Diagnosis

Diabetes is diagnosed with blood tests that measure blood glucose, such as a fasting blood glucose test or an HbA1c test, which reflects average blood glucose over the past two to three months. A health professional interprets these results.

## Prevention and management

Type 2 diabetes can often be prevented or delayed by maintaining a healthy body weight, being physically active, eating a healthy diet, and avoiding tobacco. For people who have diabetes, management includes healthy eating, regular activity, monitoring blood glucose, and, when needed, medicines including insulin. Good control of blood glucose, blood pressure, and cholesterol reduces the risk of complications.
