# Cholesterol levels

Cholesterol is a waxy, fat-like substance found in all the cells of the body. The body needs some cholesterol to work properly, but having too much of certain types in the blood can raise the risk of heart disease and stroke. Cholesterol travels through the blood attached to proteins, forming particles called lipoproteins.

## Types of cholesterol

LDL (low-density lipoprotein) is often called "bad" cholesterol because a high level leads to a buildup of cholesterol in the arteries. HDL (high-density lipoprotein) is often called "good" cholesterol because it carries cholesterol back to the liver, which removes it from the body. Triglycerides are another type of fat in the blood; high levels, especially combined with high LDL or low HDL, can raise the risk of heart disease.

## What the numbers mean

Cholesterol is measured with a blood test called a lipid panel, usually reported in mg/dL. As a general guide for adults, a total cholesterol below 200 mg/dL is considered desirable, LDL below 100 mg/dL is optimal, and an HDL of 60 mg/dL or higher is protective. Target levels can differ depending on a person's overall risk of heart disease, so results should be interpreted by a health professional.

## Lowering high cholesterol

Lifestyle steps that can help lower cholesterol include eating foods low in saturated fat, staying physically active, maintaining a healthy weight, and not smoking. When lifestyle changes are not enough, a health professional may prescribe medicines such as statins. Because high cholesterol usually has no symptoms, regular testing is the way to know your levels.
